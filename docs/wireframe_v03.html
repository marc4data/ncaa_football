Degraded, not blocked — these render today with one hole

  • Today and Matchup — srv_today_edges and srv_matchup do not exist yet, so both pages render their schedule content and show an empty state where predictions go. Enrichment, not a blocker.
  • Line Movement — the snapshot table exists and the DAG runs, but there is not yet enough history to draw a line. It fills itself in.
  • Excel Export — the workbook builds, but sheets sourced from blocked tables are omitted rather than shipped empty.

Standing constraints this wireframe assumes

  • The site reads serving and nothing else. No page touches raw, staging or marts — enforced by search_path, the cfdb_read role and the CI layering guard.
  • No joins and no metric maths in Streamlit. Every page is a single-table SELECT … WHERE. If a page needs two things joined, that is a serving-view change, not app code.
  • Team colour is identity chrome, never a data encoding. Value is encoded by position, length or a sequential ramp — never by team colour.
  • Predictions carry attribution as data. cfdb's own predictions, built on a licensed training pack — never presented as official CollegeFootballData.com predictions.

What the slider doesn't change. Moving the threshold down surfaces more rows; it doesn't make them profitable. The second slider — minimum bucket hit rate, defaulted to the 52.4% breakeven — is the one that actually protects you, and the n column is why: a 17.9-point edge on n=11 is noise wearing a big number. Keep both sliders, and keep n visible next to every hit rate.
Out-of-sample flag — new in v0.3, and mandatory. Every hit rate on this page comes from the 2025 held-out test split, not from live betting. A backtest hit rate and a realised hit rate must never render in identical styling. Each row carries is_out_of_sample from fct_prediction, and backtest-only rows are chipped – b/test. Once the season starts, live rows appear alongside and the chip is what separates them.
De-vig disclosure. market_implied_home_win_probability is blank in every model export, so it is derived by multiplicative normalisation — implied_home = (1/home_dec) / ((1/home_dec) + (1/away_dec)). The method is stored per row in devig_method, so the choice is auditable and a second method can be computed later without rewriting history. Raw moneylines in fct_betting_line are never overwritten. The assumption — vig proportional to implied probability — is stated on Methodology.

Methodology

renders — static

Front of house. Explains what the site is and what it can't do. The operational detail moved to System Overview.

Architecture
CFBD → Airflow → Databricks → dbt → Postgres → Streamlit
+ 4-hourly line snapshot DAG
Sections
  • What the model predicts — team scores → margin → spread and total, with confidence bands
  • Features — 83 opponent-adjusted metrics, as-of-week, no leakage; walk-forward validation
  • Current performance — links to Model Performance, drawdowns included
  • Known limitations — single-provider lines, no juice on spread/total, no props, line history starts [DAG date], standings tiebreakers are ours, no QBR
  • Scope — FBS plus non-FBS opponents as stubs; 2024–2026 play-by-play
  • Attribution — CollegeFootballData.com; keys server-side; no raw-data redistribution
The honest framing. While the model trails the market, the claim to make here isn't "I built a winning model" — it's "I built the instrumentation that proves whether a model wins." That's a more senior claim and a much harder one to poke holes in.

D3 — new evidence, and it changes my answer. You asked which file I meant: it's your own CFDB_Performance_Monitor 20251018 2300.xlsx, columns S (Formatted Spread) and T (Spread). I re-analysed it, and this is not a definition error:
  • The difference is symmetric noise, not a systematic offset — median 0.0, mean −0.08, std 1.57, with 75 positive and 95 negative disagreements.
  • Both predict the actual result equally well — MAE vs actual margin 11.241 using Spread, 11.219 using Formatted Spread. Neither is garbage; both are plausible market lines.
  • Formatted Spread is a half-point in 216 of 216 rows; Spread in only 120 of 216.
That pattern says two captures of the same market at different times, or two providers — most likely one column pulled when the prediction was made and the other refreshed at extract time. Worth noting CFBD's GameLine carries both spread and formattedSpread, and from a single response those two agree — so the divergence points at the workbook assembling from two pulls rather than at CFBD.

What to do: don't pick a winner yet. Once the snapshot DAG runs you'll have real timestamps, and the right fix is to store line-with-timestamp and let the site choose (line at prediction time for edge, closing line for CLV). Meanwhile: worst offenders were UTSA @ North Texas (8.5 pts), Oklahoma State @ Arizona (5.0), South Alabama @ Troy (5.0).
